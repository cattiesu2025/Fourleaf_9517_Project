\begin{abstract}
Fine-grained species recognition is difficult when classes differ only in
small morphological details and only a few labelled examples are available.
We study a fixed 500-class subset of iNaturalist 2021 under a common data split
and evaluation protocol.  With 40 training images per class, we compare
handcrafted HOG and SIFT pipelines, a ResNet18 trained from random
initialization, and frozen or fully fine-tuned ImageNet representations.  We
also examine augmentation strength, regularization, added self-attention, and
visual explanations.  Full fine-tuning obtains 66.24\% Top-1 accuracy and
66.20\% Macro-F1, improving over the best scratch model by 28.30 and 28.69
percentage points, respectively.  Basic augmentation is more effective than
either no augmentation or a stronger policy, while adding randomly initialized
self-attention does not improve generalization.  In a separate extension that
increases the cleaned training set from 20,000 to 129,378 images while keeping
the validation and test sets fixed, fine-tuning reaches 81.58\% Top-1 compared
with 64.20\% for scratch training under the same 15-epoch budget.  These
results show that pretrained representations remain valuable as data scale
increases, while augmentation and model capacity must be matched to the
available supervision.
\end{abstract}

\subsection{Evaluation and Analysis Protocol}
\label{sec:evaluation_protocol}

All methods are evaluated on the same held-out test split of 5,000 images,
containing ten images from each of 500 classes.  Model selection and
hyperparameter decisions use only the validation split; the test split is used
for final evaluation, fixed-model robustness testing, and error analysis.  We
report Top-1 and Top-5 accuracy, macro-averaged precision, recall and F1, and
balanced accuracy.  Macro-averaged metrics give equal weight to every class
and therefore complement the sample-level accuracy measures.  Scores are
recomputed from each method's saved score matrix rather than copied from
training logs.  The evaluation program additionally verifies that predictions
contain exactly 5,000 unique image identifiers, that score columns cover the
shared 500-class mapping, and that the image-to-label mapping is identical
across all submitted runs.

Training and inference times are retained as descriptive efficiency measures.
Because the limited-data methods were run on different CPU or GPU systems,
their times do not constitute a controlled hardware benchmark.  The full-data
scratch and fine-tuned runs used the same NVIDIA L40S GPU model and software
environment but different physical nodes, so their elapsed times are more
closely, though still only approximately, comparable.

Robustness is measured by applying four degradations to the decoded RGB test
images before model-specific preprocessing: additive Gaussian noise, Gaussian
blur, brightness reduction, and JPEG compression.  Each degradation is tested
at five severities.  No parameters are updated during this evaluation.  We
report performance at each severity and the change from the corresponding
same-environment, undegraded severity-0 result.  Grad-CAM is used separately
for qualitative failure analysis;
its heat maps indicate spatial sensitivity but are not treated as causal
explanations.

\subsection{Main Comparison}
\label{sec:main_comparison}

Table~\ref{tab:main_results} compares the method families in the limited-data
setting.  The handcrafted pipelines perform poorly at this scale: SIFT with
BoVW and an SVM reaches 2.94\% Top-1, whereas HOG with a random forest reaches
1.38\%.  Training ResNet18 from scratch raises Top-1 to 37.94\%, showing the
benefit of learned visual representations even with limited labels.

ImageNet pretraining produces the largest gains.  Freezing the pretrained
backbone and learning only the classifier obtains 53.88\% Top-1, 15.94
percentage points above the best scratch result.  Updating the full network
further increases Top-1 to 66.24\% and Macro-F1 to 66.20\%.  This is a 12.36
point Top-1 improvement over the frozen strategy and a 28.30 point improvement
over scratch training.  The similar values of accuracy, balanced accuracy, and
Macro-F1 are consistent with the balanced ten-image-per-class test split.

\subsection{Full-Data Scaling Extension}
\label{sec:full_data_results}

The full-data experiment isolates initialization under a matched training
configuration.  Both ResNet18 models use the cleaned 129,378-image training
set, the same fixed validation and test splits, basic augmentation, AdamW with
cosine scheduling, a batch size of 64, and a 15-epoch budget.  The scratch
model reaches 64.20\% Top-1 and 64.02\% Macro-F1.  With ImageNet
initialization and full fine-tuning, these rise to 81.58\% and 81.53\%, gains
of 17.38 and 17.52 percentage points.  Top-5 accuracy improves from 84.76\% to
93.70\%.

The fine-tuned job takes 1,351.7 seconds compared with 1,295.0 seconds for
scratch training, an increase of 56.7 seconds (4.38\%).  Since the jobs ran on
different nodes equipped with the same L40S GPU model, this small difference
should not be interpreted as a strict algorithmic overhead.  The best
validation accuracies occur at epochs 15 and 13 for scratch and fine-tuning,
respectively.  Figure~\ref{fig:full_data_curves} shows that the test advantage
is accompanied by stronger validation performance throughout training.

This extension must be distinguished from a pure limited-versus-full data
ablation.  The best limited-data scratch baseline uses 100 epochs and SGD,
whereas the full-data scratch model uses 15 epochs and AdamW.  The increase
from 37.94\% to 64.20\% is therefore descriptive and cannot be attributed only
to the additional images.  The full-data pair itself is controlled more
closely and supports the narrower conclusion that ImageNet initialization
retains a substantial advantage at the larger training scale.

\subsection{Robustness under Image Degradation}
\label{sec:robustness_results}

Figure~\ref{fig:robustness} reports the complete four-degradation by
five-severity evaluation.  The fine-tuned model has the highest absolute
Top-1 accuracy in every degraded condition, but its margin varies substantially
with the corruption.  Blur is the most damaging: at severity 5, fine-tuned
Top-1 falls from 66.24\% to 9.02\%, while scratch falls from 37.94\% to
3.90\%.  The fine-tuned model is much less affected by brightness reduction,
retaining 59.92\% Top-1 at factor 0.5 compared with 16.70\% for scratch.  At
the strongest Gaussian noise and JPEG settings it reaches 44.24\% and 50.12\%,
respectively, versus 27.70\% and 27.98\% for scratch.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{report/generated/robustness_curves.pdf}
  \caption{Top-1 accuracy under four test-time degradations.  Severity 0 is
  undegraded inference in the same local environment as severities 1--5.}
  \label{fig:robustness}
\end{figure}

Relative retention gives a more nuanced comparison.  Fine-tuning retains
13.6\%, 90.5\%, 66.8\%, and 75.7\% of its clean accuracy under maximum blur,
brightness reduction, noise, and JPEG compression.  The corresponding scratch
retention is 10.3\%, 44.0\%, 73.0\%, and 73.7\%; scratch is therefore slightly
better only for maximum Gaussian noise when normalized by its lower clean
baseline.  HOG and SIFT show smaller absolute drops largely because their
clean accuracies are already close to chance.  For these methods, noise is most
damaging to HOG (1.32\% to 0.60\%) and blur to SIFT (3.18\% to 1.26\%).

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The limited-data results answer the central representation question clearly.
Handcrafted descriptors do not capture enough of the color, texture, shape,
and contextual variation needed to separate 500 species, while a convolutional
model can learn a much stronger representation.  However, 40 images per class
remain insufficient for scratch training to approach the pretrained models.
The frozen result indicates that generic ImageNet features already transfer to
this task; the additional 12.36-point gain from fine-tuning shows that adapting
the representation to species-specific cues is also important.

The scratch and transfer ablations show that more variation or capacity is not
automatically beneficial.  Basic augmentation raises scratch Top-1 from
22.62\% to 31.76\%, whereas the strong policy reaches only 25.88\%.  A plausible
interpretation is that aggressive transformations sometimes disturb color,
texture, or local morphology that distinguishes related species.  Likewise,
regularization reduces the reported train--validation gap with only a small
Top-1 cost, but adding self-attention lowers Top-1 from 66.24\% to 62.08\%.
This is consistent with the randomly initialized attention parameters adding
capacity without enough supervision, although a single configuration cannot
establish a general rule about attention architectures.

Robustness does not reduce to the clean ranking.  Fine-tuning remains the most
accurate method under every tested corruption and is particularly stable to
brightness reduction, yet scratch preserves a slightly larger fraction of its
clean accuracy under strong Gaussian noise.  Blur removes spatial detail that
both deep models rely on and causes the steepest decline.  The apparently flat
traditional curves should not be interpreted as strong robustness, because
both methods begin near the 0.2\% chance level for 500 classes.

The full-data extension adds an important qualification.  Increasing the
training set makes scratch ResNet18 substantially more competitive, but it does
not remove the benefit of pretraining: the matched fine-tuned run retains a
17.38-point Top-1 advantage.  The smaller gap than in the limited-data main
comparison is compatible with scratch learning improving as supervision grows,
but the limited and full scratch configurations are not identical and the
change in gap should not be interpreted as a controlled scaling law.

Several limitations constrain the conclusions.  The study uses one balanced
500-class subset of iNaturalist 2021, one principal backbone, and generally one
training seed per configuration.  It therefore does not quantify run-to-run
variance or establish that the observed rankings transfer to other datasets or
architectures.  Limited-data runtimes were measured on heterogeneous hardware.
The full-data experiment covers only two initialization strategies under one
15-epoch budget, and its jobs ran on different L40S nodes.  Finally, Grad-CAM is
a qualitative localization tool rather than evidence of causal reasoning.
Future work should repeat key runs with multiple seeds, evaluate modern
fine-grained backbones and taxonomic supervision, and extend the matched
full-data ablations to frozen, regularized, and attention variants.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Under a unified 500-class evaluation protocol, pretrained ResNet18
representations outperform handcrafted features and scratch training when only
40 labelled images are available per class.  Full fine-tuning achieves 66.24\%
Top-1 in this setting, while the augmentation and capacity ablations show that
stronger perturbations or additional randomly initialized modules need not
improve generalization.  In the separate 129,378-image extension, matched
fine-tuning reaches 81.58\% Top-1 compared with 64.20\% for scratch training,
so pretraining remains useful at the larger scale.  Together, the experiments
support matching representation, augmentation strength, and model capacity to
the amount of available supervision.  Fine-tuning also remains the most
accurate method under every tested degradation, although blur exposes a shared
dependence on fine spatial detail.  Robustness and visual failure analysis
therefore provide complementary evidence about where the resulting models
still fail.
